## 1. PURPOSE

This procedure defines the response to security incidents and personal-data breaches affecting Hidden History.

A personal-data breach can include accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to personal data.

---

## 2. INCIDENT REPORTING

Security or privacy incidents should be reported immediately to:

SovereignSoftwareLtd@gmail.com

If the incident concerns a production provider, the relevant provider incident process must also be followed.

---

## 3. FIRST RESPONSE

The priority is to:

1. Establish what happened.
2. Contain the incident.
3. Protect affected information.
4. Preserve relevant evidence.
5. Assess whether personal data is involved.
6. Assess risk to affected individuals.
7. Determine notification requirements.
8. Record the incident.

---

## 4. PERSONAL-DATA BREACH ASSESSMENT

For each suspected personal-data breach, assess:

- Nature of the breach
- Categories of personal data
- Number of individuals affected
- Likely consequences
- Severity of consequences
- Likelihood of harm
- Measures already taken
- Measures available to reduce harm

---

## 5. ICO NOTIFICATION

Where a personal-data breach is likely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms, Hidden History must notify the ICO without undue delay and, where feasible, within **72 hours of becoming aware of the breach**.

The 72-hour period runs from awareness of the breach, not from the time the incident originally occurred.

If all information is not available within 72 hours, the notification should not be unnecessarily delayed. Further information can be supplied later where appropriate.

---

## 6. HIGH-RISK BREACHES

Where a personal-data breach is likely to result in a high risk to individuals' rights and freedoms, affected individuals must be informed without undue delay where the applicable legal requirements require this.

The communication should explain:

- What happened
- The likely consequences
- Measures taken
- Measures users can take
- Contact information

---

## 7. RECORD ALL BREACHES

Every personal-data breach must be recorded, whether or not it is notified to the ICO.

The record should include:

- Facts of the breach
- Effects
- Risk assessment
- Decision
- Notifications
- Remedial action
- Lessons learned

---

## 8. CONTAINMENT

Possible actions include:

- Revoking compromised credentials
- Resetting credentials
- Restricting access
- Disabling affected endpoints
- Correcting access policies
- Removing exposed data
- Blocking malicious activity
- Contacting providers
- Preserving evidence before destructive remediation where appropriate

---

## 9. PROVIDER INCIDENTS

Where Supabase, Google, Gemini, eBay or another provider reports an incident, Hidden History must assess:

- Whether Hidden History data was involved
- Whether Hidden History is the controller for that data
- The affected users/data
- Provider remediation
- ICO notification requirements
- User notification requirements

---

## 10. SECURITY INCIDENTS WITHOUT PERSONAL-DATA BREACH

A security incident may not involve personal data.

Examples may include:

- Service availability failure
- Attempted abuse
- Denial-of-service activity
- Non-personal technical compromise
- Attempted unauthorised access that did not expose data

Such incidents should still be investigated and recorded where material.

---

## 11. EVIDENCE

Preserve relevant evidence such as:

- Timestamps
- System events
- Security records
- Provider notifications
- Relevant configuration changes
- Screenshots
- Communications

Do not retain unnecessary personal information merely for incident curiosity.

---

## 12. POST-INCIDENT REVIEW

After a significant incident:

- Identify root cause
- Identify control failures
- Implement corrective actions
- Review access controls
- Review provider controls
- Update risk assessments
- Record lessons learned
