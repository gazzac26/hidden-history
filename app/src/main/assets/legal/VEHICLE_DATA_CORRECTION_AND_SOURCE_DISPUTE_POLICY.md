1. PURPOSE

This policy explains how Hidden History handles concerns about the accuracy of vehicle information and analysis.

It distinguishes between:

1. information originating from an external vehicle-data source;
2. information received and processed by Hidden History;
3. information displayed by Hidden History; and
4. analysis generated by Hidden History from available information.

The purpose is to ensure that suspected errors are investigated appropriately and that Hidden History does not claim the ability to alter records controlled by another organisation.

---

2. CURRENT VEHICLE-DATA SOURCES

At launch, Hidden History may retrieve vehicle information from:

- Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency (DVLA); and
- Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA), including MOT information.

Additional authorised data sources may be introduced in the future.

A future data source must not be represented as connected until it has been legally and technically authorised and implemented.

---

3. SOURCE DATA

Hidden History may receive information from an external source and may normalise, structure, combine or display that information within the application.

The underlying source record remains controlled by the relevant source authority or provider.

Hidden History does not automatically become the owner or controller of an external source's underlying vehicle record merely because the information is displayed or processed by Hidden History.

Where an external source is responsible for correcting the underlying record, the user may need to contact that source directly.

---

4. DISTINGUISHING SOURCE ERRORS FROM HIDDEN HISTORY ERRORS

When an error is reported, Hidden History should determine, where reasonably possible, whether the issue relates to:

- the original source record;
- information returned by the source;
- Hidden History's storage or processing;
- data mapping or normalisation;
- display or formatting;
- association with the wrong vehicle or report; or
- Hidden History's analysis of the available information.

The distinction is important because Hidden History may be able to correct its own processing or presentation but may not be able to amend an underlying external record.

---

5. SOURCE RECORD CORRECTIONS

Where the underlying record is controlled by DVLA, DVSA or another external source, Hidden History will not represent itself as having authority to alter that source's record unless it actually has that authority.

Users may be directed to the relevant source or authority where a correction to the underlying record is required.

For example, DVSA provides procedures for correcting certain errors in MOT records, including mileage and vehicle details, and provides a process for missing or incorrect MOT test records.

Where a user disputes the result of an MOT test itself, DVSA also provides an appeal process.

---

6. HIDDEN HISTORY PROCESSING ERRORS

If Hidden History incorrectly:

- displays information;
- maps information;
- formats information;
- associates information with the wrong vehicle;
- associates information with the wrong saved report;
- stores information incorrectly;
- interprets available information incorrectly; or
- produces an analysis that does not correctly reflect the information available to it,

the issue should be investigated by Hidden History.

Where an error is confirmed, Hidden History should take reasonable steps to correct it.

Where the affected information constitutes personal data, applicable data-protection accuracy and rectification requirements will also apply.

---

7. REPORTING AN ERROR

Users may report suspected errors by contacting:

SovereignSoftwareLtd@gmail.com

Where reasonably available, users should provide:

- the account email associated with the report, where applicable;
- vehicle registration;
- report or search date, where known;
- description of the suspected error;
- screenshot or copy of the relevant information, where appropriate;
- relevant supporting evidence; and
- details of the original source or document where known.

Users should avoid sending unnecessary personal information.

Hidden History may request additional information where reasonably necessary to investigate the reported issue.

---

8. INVESTIGATION

Where appropriate, Hidden History should determine:

1. what information was received from the relevant source;
2. when it was received;
3. what information was retained, where applicable;
4. how the information was processed;
5. what information was displayed;
6. whether the information was associated with the correct vehicle and report;
7. whether any analysis was generated from the information;
8. whether the reported information is inaccurate, incomplete or misleading;
9. whether the issue originated with the external source or Hidden History; and
10. what corrective action, if any, is reasonably possible.

The investigation should take into account the evidence supplied by the user and the importance of accuracy for the purpose for which the information is used.

The ICO expects organisations to carefully consider challenges to the accuracy of personal data and maintain appropriate processes for addressing them.

---

9. SOURCE-DATA DISPUTES

Where the underlying government or external record is disputed, Hidden History may explain that it is displaying information supplied by the relevant source and may direct the user to that source's correction or appeal process.

Hidden History should not:

- claim that an external record is wrong without appropriate evidence;
- claim authority to amend another organisation's records where none exists; or
- present a user's allegation about an external record as an established fact.

Where appropriate, Hidden History may record that the accuracy of information has been challenged while the matter is being investigated.

---

10. ANALYSIS CORRECTIONS

An analysis result is derived from the information available to Hidden History at the relevant time.

If Hidden History identifies that an analysis result was incorrect because it:

- used the wrong vehicle information;
- incorrectly interpreted available information;
- incorrectly mapped data;
- incorrectly applied its analysis methodology; or
- otherwise generated an incorrect result,

the issue should be reviewed and corrected where appropriate.

A correction to analysis does not necessarily mean that the underlying source record was incorrect.

Similarly, an underlying source correction does not necessarily mean that an earlier Hidden History analysis was incorrect based on the information available when it was generated.

---

11. OPINION, INTERPRETATION AND ANALYSIS

Not every disagreement with a Hidden History result constitutes a factual data error.

Where an output involves interpretation, risk assessment or analysis rather than a direct statement of fact, Hidden History should distinguish:

- the underlying source information;
- the factual information displayed;
- the methodology applied; and
- the resulting analysis or interpretation.

Where applicable, users may challenge the underlying information or the way Hidden History processed it.

Hidden History will not represent an analytical conclusion as an external authority's factual determination.

---

12. HISTORICAL SAVED REPORTS

A saved report may represent the information and analysis available at the time the report was generated.

If the underlying source subsequently changes, the original saved report may remain a historical snapshot of what was available at that time.

Where appropriate, Hidden History may provide or generate a refreshed result rather than silently rewriting the historical record.

The actual behaviour will depend on the production implementation of saved reports.

Where a saved report includes a generation timestamp, that timestamp identifies when the report was generated.

---

13. CORRECTION OF SAVED REPORTS

If Hidden History identifies that a saved report contains an error caused by Hidden History's own processing, it may:

- correct the stored information;
- correct the report data;
- mark the affected information as corrected;
- regenerate the report; or
- take another appropriate corrective action.

The appropriate action will depend on the nature of the error and the actual report functionality.

Where a historical record needs to remain available for evidential purposes, Hidden History may preserve the historical record while clearly identifying the correction or subsequent finding where appropriate.

---

14. DATA-PROTECTION RECTIFICATION

Where a reported issue concerns personal data held by Hidden History, the applicable data-protection rights and procedures also apply.

Individuals have a right to request correction of inaccurate personal data and, where appropriate, completion of incomplete personal data.

Requests will be handled in accordance with the Hidden History privacy and privacy-request procedures.

The ICO's current guidance states that organisations should take reasonable steps to establish whether challenged personal data is accurate and rectify it where necessary.

Where accuracy is being investigated, applicable restriction-of-processing requirements will also be considered.

---

15. NO ALTERATION OF EXTERNAL RECORDS

Hidden History will not alter, fabricate or otherwise manipulate an external government or third-party vehicle record in order to produce a preferred result.

Where information is corrected by the original source, Hidden History may use the corrected information when it next retrieves or processes the relevant data, subject to the actual data-source arrangement.

---

16. COMMUNICATION OF OUTCOME

Following an investigation, Hidden History should communicate the outcome where reasonably appropriate.

The outcome may explain:

- that the information matched the external source;
- that a Hidden History processing or display error was identified;
- that the information was incomplete;
- that the source record requires correction by the relevant authority;
- that additional evidence is required; or
- that the issue could not be conclusively determined.

Hidden History should avoid making unsupported findings about disputed external records.

---

17. EVIDENCE AND RECORD KEEPING

Where reasonably necessary, Hidden History may retain limited records of reported accuracy issues and investigations for accountability, security, legal or operational purposes.

Any such retention must be proportionate and handled in accordance with the applicable Hidden History data-retention and privacy policies.

Where a challenge concerns personal data, appropriate records of the decision and reasoning may be maintained where required or reasonably necessary for accountability.

---

18. FUTURE DATA SOURCES

Additional authorised vehicle-data sources may be introduced in the future.

When a new source is introduced, Hidden History should review:

- source ownership;
- correction procedures;
- data permissions;
- contractual restrictions;
- technical integration;
- source reliability;
- correction responsibilities; and
- applicable privacy and legal requirements.

No future source should be treated as a current source until formally implemented.

---
