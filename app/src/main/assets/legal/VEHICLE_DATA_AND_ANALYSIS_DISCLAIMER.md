Hidden History may obtain vehicle information from connected third-party or official data sources.

- subject to source limitations; or

3. OFFICIAL RECORDS

Where Hidden History displays information obtained from an official source, the information should be understood as information recorded or supplied by that source.

Hidden History's display, organisation, interpretation or analysis of such information does not alter the underlying official record.

Where a user believes an official vehicle record is incorrect, the user may need to contact the relevant source or authority directly.

For example, DVSA provides procedures for correcting certain errors in MOT records.

4. MOT INFORMATION

An MOT result records the outcome of a particular test.

It should not be treated as a guarantee of the vehicle's current condition.

GOV.UK states that a vehicle can be unsafe even when it has a current MOT certificate.

A vehicle may develop faults after an MOT has been completed.

Similarly:

- a vehicle that passed an MOT may subsequently develop faults;
- a vehicle with advisories may have had those items repaired;
- a vehicle that previously failed may have subsequently been repaired and passed; and
- the condition of a vehicle can change between tests.

---

5. LIMITATIONS OF THE MOT TEST

The MOT is a statutory vehicle test against specified requirements.

It is not a comprehensive mechanical inspection.

For example, GOV.UK states that the MOT test does not cover the condition of the engine, clutch or gearbox.

Users should therefore not interpret an MOT pass as confirmation that every mechanical component of a vehicle is in good condition.

---

6. MOT FAILURES AND ADVISORIES

Where available, Hidden History may display historical MOT:

- passes;
- failures;
- mileage readings;
- advisories;
- minor defects; and
- other information returned through the applicable DVSA service.

These records are historical information relating to recorded tests.

The presence of a historical failure or advisory does not necessarily establish that the issue remains present.

The absence of a historical failure or advisory does not establish that the vehicle is free from defects.

Users should consider the full history alongside a current physical inspection and other appropriate checks.

GOV.UK provides access to MOT history including recorded mileage and, for applicable vehicles, details of failures and minor problems.
