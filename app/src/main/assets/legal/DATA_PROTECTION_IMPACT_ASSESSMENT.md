1. PURPOSE

This Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) documents the personal-data processing undertaken by Hidden History and assesses the potential risks that processing may create for the privacy and other data-protection rights of individuals.

The DPIA records:

- The personal data processed
- The purposes for which it is processed
- The systems and third parties involved
- The necessity and proportionality of the processing
- The principal risks to individuals
- The safeguards used to reduce those risks
- Any additional measures required
- The circumstances in which this DPIA must be reviewed

Hidden History applies data protection by design and by default and seeks to minimise personal-data collection and retention.

This DPIA is maintained as part of the Hidden History accountability framework and will be reviewed when processing materially changes.

5. PERSONAL DATA PROCESSED

5.1 Account and authentication data

Hidden History may process:

- Email address
- Authentication/user identifier
- Profile information supplied by the user
- Account status
- Pro entitlement or purchase-related entitlement information

This information is used to provide authenticated account functionality and manage access to services purchased through Google Play.

---

5.2 Vehicle-search information

When a user performs a vehicle search, Hidden History may process:

- Vehicle registration number
- Vehicle information returned by official vehicle-data sources
- MOT information
- MOT mileage readings
- MOT advisories
- MOT failures
- Search/report generation timestamps

An ordinary vehicle search is not retained as a permanent search-history record merely because the user performed the search.

Where the user chooses to save a report, the relevant report information is stored against the user's account.

---

5.3 Saved vehicle reports

A saved vehicle report may contain:

- Vehicle information
- Registration information
- MOT information
- Vehicle analysis information
- Advert analysis information where applicable
- Report generation timestamp
- User/account association

Saved reports are provided for the user's own access and use.

Users can delete saved reports.

When an account is deleted, account-linked reports and other account data are deleted subject to any limited retention that may be legally necessary.

---

5.4 Advert information

Users may submit vehicle advertisement content for analysis.

Advert information may contain information relating to individuals, including information that a seller has placed into an advertisement.

Hidden History does not intentionally request unnecessary personal information from advertisements.

Where personal information appears within an advertisement supplied by a user, Hidden History processes that information only insofar as necessary for the requested analysis and operation of the service.

Advert analysis is concerned primarily with:

- Vehicle information
- Advert claims
- Advert wording
- Potential inconsistencies
- Potential vehicle-related risks

The analysis is not intended to profile the seller or make decisions about an individual.

---

5.5 Technical and security information

Hidden History and its infrastructure may generate limited technical or security information necessary to operate and protect the service.

This may include information such as:

- Authentication/session information
- Security records
- Error information
- Diagnostic information
- Technical information generated by service infrastructure

Hidden History does not operate a separate analytics or advertising-tracking system.

Technical information is retained only where reasonably necessary for security, operation, troubleshooting, legal compliance or another documented purpose.

10. NECESSITY AND PROPORTIONALITY

Hidden History follows the principle of data minimisation.

The application should only collect and process personal information that is adequate, relevant and reasonably necessary for the stated purpose.

In particular:

- Registration numbers are processed because they are required to retrieve vehicle information.
- Account information is processed because authenticated functionality requires an identifiable account.
- Saved-report information is retained because the user has specifically chosen to save the report.
- Advert information is processed because the user has requested advert analysis.
- Technical/security information is processed only where required for service operation, security, troubleshooting or another documented purpose.
- Hidden History does not retain unsaved searches as a permanent search-history database.

The ICO identifies data minimisation as a core data-protection principle and requires personal data to be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary.

---

11. RETENTION AND STORAGE LIMITATION

Hidden History has a deliberately limited-retention approach.

Unsaved searches

Ordinary vehicle searches are not retained as permanent user search history.

---

Saved reports

Saved reports remain available to the user until:

- The user deletes the report; or
- The user's account is deleted,

subject to any limited retention required by law or necessary for security.

---

Account information

Account information is retained while the account remains active and for only as long afterwards as is reasonably necessary for a documented legal or security purpose.

---

Technical/security information

Technical and security information is retained only for as long as reasonably necessary for the purpose for which it was created.

---

Legally required information

Where information must legally be retained for a particular period, Hidden History will retain only the information required and will restrict access to it appropriately.

Hidden History does not adopt arbitrary retention periods simply for convenience or future commercial use.

---

12. SAVED REPORT TIMESTAMPS

Saved reports contain a report-generation timestamp.

The timestamp is stored as a date/time value and allows the user and Hidden History to identify when the report was generated.

This supports:

- Report identification
- User transparency
- Report history
- Support enquiries
- Verification of when information was retrieved

The timestamp does not create a separate permanent search-history system for unsaved searches.

---

13. PDF REPORTS

When a user downloads or shares a report, Hidden History may generate a PDF within the application.

The generated PDF includes the report timestamp.

Hidden History does not retain the generated PDF as a permanent copy on its servers.

The PDF is a user-generated output provided to the user for download or sharing.

Once generated, the user controls the resulting file outside Hidden History's server-side storage.

16. AUTOMATED ANALYSIS AND DECISION-MAKING

Hidden History performs automated analysis of vehicle and advertisement information.

The purpose is to assist the user in researching a vehicle and identifying potential risks or inconsistencies.

The analysis concerns:

- Vehicle characteristics
- Vehicle records
- MOT information
- Advertisement information
- Advert claims

Hidden History does not use this analysis to make decisions producing legal effects or similarly significant effects concerning individuals.

The system therefore operates as a vehicle-information and decision-support service for the user rather than an automated decision-making system concerning individuals.

20. SECURITY RISKS

Risk 1 — Unauthorised account access

An attacker could gain access to a user's account and potentially access saved reports or account information.

Controls:

- Secure authentication
- Server-side authorisation
- Database access controls
- Row-level security where applicable
- Secure session handling
- Restricted backend access
- Appropriate security monitoring

---

Risk 2 — Unauthorised access to saved reports

Saved reports are associated with user accounts.

Controls:

- User authentication
- Account-level authorisation
- Database security policies
- Server-side access control
- Report deletion functionality

---

Risk 3 — Unauthorised Pro access

A user may attempt to bypass Pro restrictions.

Controls:

- Server-side entitlement verification
- Authentication
- Server-controlled access
- No reliance solely on client-side restrictions

---

Risk 4 — Exposure of government API credentials

Government-data credentials could create significant security risk if exposed through the Android application.

Controls:

- Credentials remain server-side.
- Credentials are not embedded in the Android application.
- Protected API access is performed through server-side infrastructure.
- Secrets are not intentionally exposed to application users.

---

Risk 5 — Third-party compromise

A compromise of an external processor could expose information processed through that provider.

Controls:

- Data minimisation
- Limited data sharing
- Appropriate provider due diligence
- Contractual safeguards where required
- Restricted access
- Review of processor security arrangements

---

Risk 6 — Excessive retention

Data retained unnecessarily increases the impact of any security incident.

Controls:

- No permanent retention of unsaved searches
- User-controlled saved-report deletion
- Account deletion
- Limited technical/security retention
- Formal retention documentation
- Periodic review of stored information

---

25. DPIA RISK ASSESSMENT

Identified risks

The principal identified privacy risks are:

1. Unauthorised account access
2. Unauthorised access to saved reports
3. Unauthorised Pro access
4. Exposure of backend/API credentials
5. Unauthorised access to third-party processed information
6. Excessive data retention
7. Incorrect or disputed vehicle information
8. Potential exposure of personal information contained within third-party advertisements
9. International-transfer risks where applicable

Residual risk

Overall residual privacy risk: LOW to MEDIUM

The residual risk is considered manageable because:

- Hidden History processes a limited range of personal data.
- The service is not designed around personal profiling.
- No advertising or behavioural tracking is used.
- No analytics platform is used.
- Unsaved vehicle searches are not retained as permanent search history.
- Saved reports are user-controlled.
- Users can delete reports and accounts.
- Vehicle analysis concerns vehicles and advertisements rather than individuals.
- Special category data is not intentionally collected.
- Security controls are applied to account and saved-report access.

The residual risk must nevertheless be reviewed whenever processing changes materially.

26. NECESSITY OF DPIA

A DPIA is an accountability and risk-management document used to identify and reduce risks associated with personal-data processing.

The ICO states that a DPIA is mandatory where processing is likely to result in a high risk to individuals' rights and freedoms, and recommends DPIA use as a good-practice tool more broadly.

Hidden History maintains this DPIA because it provides a documented assessment of the service's processing, safeguards and residual risks.

The current Hidden History processing does not intentionally involve:

- Large-scale special-category processing
- Criminal-offence-data profiling
- Systematic and extensive profiling of individuals
- Automated decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects concerning individuals

The DPIA will nevertheless remain under review because the application processes account information, saved reports, third-party information and automated vehicle/advert analysis.

28. DATA-PROTECTION-BY-DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Privacy and data protection are incorporated into the Hidden History service design.

The service is intentionally designed so that:

- Unsaved searches are not retained as permanent history.
- Personal data is not collected merely because it may be commercially useful.
- Saved reports exist because the user chooses to save them.
- Account-linked information is access controlled.
- Backend credentials are not placed in the client application.
- No advertising or behavioural-tracking infrastructure is used.
- Future functionality is not automatically treated as part of the current processing operation.

The ICO's current data-protection-by-design guidance emphasises considering privacy from the beginning of a service and implementing appropriate technical and organisational measures.

The principal privacy risks identified in this DPIA are capable of being reduced through the technical, organisational and procedural safeguards documented above.

The most important safeguards are:

- Data minimisation
- Limited retention
- User-controlled deletion
- Account security
- Server-side authorisation
- Protection of API credentials
- Processor controls
- International-transfer assessment
- Data-subject rights procedures
- Breach-response procedures
